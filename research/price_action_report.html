Price Action on NIFTY and SENSEX

What the evidence says about trading 1- to 15-minute price action on Indian index options, tested on your own 6.5 months of 1-minute data.

Prepared 25 September 2026 Data NIFTY 137 · SENSEX 139 sessions, Nov 2025 to Sep 2026 Instrument weekly options, 1 lot, buy-only

Not investment advice. This note is written to the standard of a research analyst but its author is not registered with SEBI. Every figure below is reproducible from the scripts in the repository, and every external claim carries its source. Past patterns in 137 sessions are not a forecast.

Verdict

At the 1- to 15-minute scale, NIFTY and SENSEX behave close to a fair coin. No candle pattern, opening-range breakout, structure rule or short-horizon momentum or reversal rule produced an edge that survives costs on both indices in this sample, and the peer-reviewed literature does not supply one either. The current algorithm's trigger, 15-minute momentum, loses money on its own before any of the AI layers are added.

The only effects with statistical support are structural, not directional: volatility is roughly three times higher in the first 15 minutes than at midday, large opening gaps mostly do not fill, and a single round trip costs about ₹70 to ₹90 before spread, which is more than the size of most documented intraday effects. A price-action approach can still be built, but it has to trade session-level structure, once or twice a day, with stops scaled to the hour, and it needs at least two years of data before it is trusted with money.

01What would count as evidence

A rule earns a place in a live system only if it clears four bars at once. These are the bars used throughout this note.

  1. Statistical. The mean profit per trade, after costs, must be at least two standard errors above zero (t ≥ 2). With fewer than 30 trades no rule can pass, whatever its win rate.
  2. Cross-sectional. NIFTY and SENSEX move together with a correlation above 0.9. A genuine effect must show the same sign on both. A rule that works on one and fails on the other is describing a few days, not the market.
  3. Temporal. The sign must hold in both sub-periods available here, November 2025 and April to September 2026.
  4. Parametric. Shifting a threshold or an entry time by a small amount must not flip the sign. An edge that lives at one exact setting is a coincidence that was searched for.

02Data and the cost model

The data is the 1-minute index history bundled with the project: 137 NIFTY sessions and 139 SENSEX sessions between 3 November 2025 and 17 September 2026, with a gap from December to March. Each session has all 375 bars and real open, high, low and close. Volume is present on most bars but is index volume, which carries little information.

There are no option-price bars, so option P&L is modelled: a 0.72-delta in-the-money weekly at 0.7% of spot (about ₹165 on NIFTY, ₹520 on SENSEX), premium moving linearly with spot, time decay of about 9% of premium per session, and 0.1% slippage each way. Costs use Dhan's schedule: ₹20 per order, STT on the sell premium, exchange charges, stamp duty and GST. Two things make this model generous to the strategies rather than harsh.

  • STT on options rose from 0.10% to 0.15% of sell premium on 1 April 2026. The model still uses 0.10%, so real costs are about ₹5 per trade higher.
  • The model has no bid-ask spread. Practitioner measurements put an all-in NIFTY round trip at ₹120 to ₹210 per lot once two spread crossings are included, against the ₹70 to ₹90 used here.

03What the literature says

Almost nothing peer-reviewed tests minute-scale price action on Indian index options. What exists is work on intraday return dynamics in US and global index products, one NIFTY futures volatility study, unaudited practitioner backtests, and SEBI's population data on retail outcomes. The table grades each effect on the strength of the evidence, not on how often it is repeated in trading content.

Two practitioner frameworks are worth stating precisely because parts of them are testable. Brooks classifies each day as trend or range by about the 18th five-minute bar and only fades extremes in ranges or buys pullbacks in trends. Wyckoff's spring is a brief penetration below a range that closes back inside within a few bars. Both are hypotheses about session-level structure, not minute-level candles, which matters for the design in section 06.

Why the current algorithm cannot work as designed. Its trigger is the mean of the last 15 one-minute returns crossing a threshold of about one point per minute. The table shows that after a large 15-minute move the next 15 minutes are, if anything, slightly negative. The algorithm then places a 6- to 15-point premium stop, about 8 to 20 spot points, against a market whose median 5-minute range in the first hour is 25 points. It is a continuation bet with a noise-sized stop in the noisiest hour of the day, taken eleven times before 10:00.

The two that looked promising

Gap fade. On NIFTY the result is stable across thresholds of 0.4% to 0.75% and entry times of 09:20 to 09:45, always between +₹27,000 and +₹68,000. On SENSEX the same grid is negative at every cell, between −₹6,000 and −₹38,000. Inside the NIFTY result, all 17 gap-up trades together lost ₹8,000 while the 17 gap-down trades made ₹65,500, and ₹45,800 of that came from nine April 2026 sessions. This is a description of buying dips during one rally, not a property of gaps.

Trend-day continuation. Positive on both indices at the 10:15 and 10:30 checks with a half-range threshold, but 16 trades each, and the same rule checked at 10:45 loses on both. A rule whose sign depends on a 15-minute timing choice has not been found; it has been fitted.

Without time decay, as if traded through futures, both improve materially (gap fade on NIFTY +₹83,900; trend day at 10:30 +₹13,600 and +₹23,000) because multi-hour holds are where theta hurts most. That is a statement about instrument choice, not about the edge.

06What this means for the algorithm

The request was a price-action system without indicators. The evidence supports building one, but only in a specific shape, and it is nearly the opposite of what exists today.

  1. Move up the timeframe. Nothing at 1 to 5 minutes clears the noise floor or the cost line on either index. Decisions should be made on 15-minute closes and session structure: the opening range, the previous day's range, the gap, and whether the day has declared itself a trend or a range by mid-morning. This is what Brooks and Wyckoff actually describe, stripped of the candle folklore.
  2. One or two trades a day, not twelve. At ₹70 to ₹90 statutory plus spread per round trip, eleven trades a day cost about ₹800 to ₹1,500 before any market risk. The current engine's gross profit over the whole sample was ₹49,000 on NIFTY; its costs were ₹98,000.
  3. Scale stops to the hour. Use the measured 5-minute range profile: a stop of roughly 1.5 times the median 15-minute move for that hour (about 20 points at 11:00 on NIFTY, 40 or more in the first half hour) rather than a fixed 6 to 15 premium points. Enter after 09:45, when half the day's range has already formed, or not at all in the first half hour.
  4. Choose the instrument for the hold time. A rule that holds until 15:15 pays a full day of theta. For session-length holds, index futures or a deep in-the-money option with delta above 0.85 keep most of the move and lose far less to decay. The buy-only weekly ATM or near-ITM option suits holds of under an hour, and nothing in this study supports holds that short.
  5. Remove what is measured to be inert or harmful. The GARCH mean trigger, the 20-point counter-trend exit on SENSEX, the leaky SVM and XGBoost vetoes, and every 1-minute candle skill. Keep the risk manager's breakeven and trailing mechanics, which are sound, once the stop distances are rescaled.
  6. Validate before trading. Two hypotheses are worth carrying forward as hypotheses: gap-day behaviour conditioned on gap direction and prior-day trend, and trend-day continuation once a day has moved beyond its opening range by mid-morning. Each needs at least three years of 1-minute data for both indices (NSE sells this), a walk-forward test with the first two years as the only fitting window, and at least 100 trades in the held-out year with t ≥ 2 on both indices. Until then they are research, not a strategy.

A candidate specification to test, not to trade. At 10:30, classify the day. If price sits beyond the 09:15 to 09:30 range by at least half that range's width, the day is a trend day: enter in that direction on the next 15-minute close that holds, stop at the far side of the opening range, exit at 15:15, one trade. If price is inside the opening range at 10:30 and the range is at least 60 NIFTY points, the day is a range day: fade the next touch of the range edge on a 15-minute close back inside, stop 0.25 range beyond the edge, target the opposite edge, at most two trades. Skip expiry days and days with a gap above 1%. This encodes the only two structural effects with any support here, and it can be fully evaluated in the existing harness in under a minute per index.

07Limitations

  • Six and a half months, not twelve, with a four-month hole. The 2026 portion is a single regime. Any conclusion about a directional effect is provisional; the conclusions about noise, costs and the volatility profile are not, because those are stable properties.
  • Option prices are modelled, not observed. Fixed delta and linear decay flatter long holds and ignore volatility crush after gaps. Real option P&L would likely be worse for the multi-hour strategies.
  • Intra-bar exits are evaluated at the adverse extreme first. This is conservative for stops and mildly pessimistic for trailing exits.
  • Around 60 rule variants were examined. With that many looks, one or two t-statistics near 1.6 are expected by chance alone, which is why bars 2 to 4 in section 01 exist.
